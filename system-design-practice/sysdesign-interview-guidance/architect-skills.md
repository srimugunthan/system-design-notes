Here's what typically distinguishes an Architect's skill set from a Senior Developer's — not a strict hierarchy, but the areas where the role tends to demand more:

## 1. System-Level Thinking Beyond a Single Codebase
- Designing for how *multiple services/systems* interact, not just how one component is built well
- Making trade-offs across the entire technology landscape (data flow, integration points, failure domains) rather than optimizing one module

## 2. Cross-Cutting Non-Functional Requirements
- Deep ownership of scalability, availability, disaster recovery, and performance *budgets* across the whole system
- Capacity planning and defining SLAs/SLOs at an organizational level, not just for one service

## 3. Technology Selection & Standardization
- Evaluating and choosing frameworks, platforms, and vendors with a multi-year horizon
- Setting technical standards, patterns, and guardrails that many teams must follow — not just picking libraries for their own project

## 4. Enterprise/Domain Architecture Awareness
- Understanding how a system fits into broader enterprise architecture (data governance, security architecture, compliance boundaries — especially relevant in regulated domains like financial services)
- Awareness of business capability models and how technical decisions map to business strategy

## 5. Trade-off Communication to Non-Technical Stakeholders
- Translating architectural decisions and risk into business language for leadership, compliance, or product stakeholders
- Producing artifacts like ADRs, architecture diagrams, and design reviews meant to align many teams, not just document a decision for one team

## 6. Long-Term Technical Debt & Evolution Strategy
- Planning migration paths, deprecation strategy, and how a system evolves over 2–5 years
- Balancing "build vs buy vs integrate" decisions at a portfolio level

## 7. Governance & Review Authority
- Running or chairing architecture review boards
- Setting and enforcing patterns (e.g., API design standards, event schemas, security baselines) across teams

## 8. Breadth Over Depth (Generalist-Specialist Balance)
- Fluency across multiple stacks/domains (data, infra, security, application) sufficient to ask the right questions and spot risks, even without hands-on mastery of every layer
- Senior developers usually go *deep* in their stack; architects trade some depth for *breadth* across the system landscape

## Where They Overlap
Both need strong technical judgment, mentoring ability, and the capacity to make sound engineering trade-offs — the difference is mostly **scope** (single system vs. multi-system/enterprise) and **audience** (engineering team vs. cross-functional stakeholders), not raw technical skill.
